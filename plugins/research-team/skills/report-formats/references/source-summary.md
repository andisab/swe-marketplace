# Source summary

Summarizes one article, paper, whitepaper, book or talk for a reader deciding whether to read it, or who wants its argument without the length. Keep the author's claims apart from your assessment. The sections below fit an article; a paper or whitepaper replaces section 3 with method, results and limitations, and a book replaces it with one short section per chapter or part, each a two-to-five-sentence summary of its claims.

## Sections

1. **Title, author, venue and date,** with a link to the source.
2. **In one paragraph.** The question the source asks and its answer.
3. **The argument.** The main claims in the order the source makes them, with the evidence given for each.
4. **Key numbers and quotes.** The figures and definitions a reader will cite later, each with its location (page, section or timestamp). Quote at most a sentence or two.
5. **Assessment.** What the source does well, what it leaves out, and who should read it. Mark this as the writer's opinion.
6. **Related sources.** Only those the research notes contain.

## Example skeleton (Markdown profile)

```markdown
# Summary: "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., NeurIPS 2017)

Source: https://…

## In one paragraph

## The argument
### Method
### Results
### Limitations

## Key numbers and quotes

## Assessment

## Related sources
```
